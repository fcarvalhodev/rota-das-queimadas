Did fires and deforestation rise under whom?

Fire hotspots in Brazil and Amazon clearing, government by government, from Lula's first term to today, with official INPE data. What each government did, what drought explains and what is people's fault. With the same standard for everyone.

📊 See the summary in icons

The short answer

It depends on which number. In Amazon deforestation, the biggest drop was under Lula 1 and 2 (from 27,800 km² in 2004 to 7,000 in 2010) and the biggest rise was under Bolsonaro (+54% between 2018 and 2022). Under Lula 3, it fell again, 51% by 2025. In fires, the highest average is Lula 1 and 2's, when clearing was at its peak. After him, the highest is Bolsonaro's and the lowest, Dilma's. In 2024, with the strongest drought on record, Brazil had the most hotspots since 2010. In 2025, the Amazon had the fewest hotspots ever measured.

Fire and deforestation are different things

They go together, but they are measured in different ways:

🔥 Fire hotspot

A heat spot the satellite sees on a given day. INPE has counted hotspots across Brazil since 1998. One large fire can become many hotspots. Drought and heat push the number up, even if nobody changes their behaviour.

🌳 Deforestation

Forest cleared for good, measured in square kilometres per year by INPE's PRODES. The “year” runs from August to July. After clearing, many people use fire to clean the area, which is why the two go together.

Average hotspots per year under each government

The years with the most fire were Lula 1 and 2: an average of 302,000 hotspots a year, at a time when Amazon clearing was also at its peak. Afterwards, the average fell under every government. Among the four that followed, the highest was Bolsonaro's, with 201,000, and the lowest, Dilma's, with 171,000. Lula 3 has 180,000 up to September 2026.

The Amazon alone

In the Amazon, the order is similar. 2024 had 140,000 hotspots, the most since 2007, with the strongest drought on record. In 2025, there were 43,000, the lowest number in the whole series, which begins in 1998.

The yearly average adds up each government's months. Lula 3 runs to September 2026; the hottest months of 2026 have passed, but the year is not over. A hotspot is a heat spot seen on one day, not a whole fire.

Drought or people's fault?

Both. Fire in the forest almost always starts with someone, to clear pasture or felled land. The climate decides how far it spreads.

What makes fire go up

  • Strong drought and heat, as in 2024, with El Niño.
  • A lot of cleared forest, which is then burned to become pasture.
  • Weak enforcement, which leaves criminal fires unpunished.

So be careful with comparisons

  • A bad year can come from a drought, without anyone changing their behaviour.
  • A drop can come from rain, not only from enforcement.
  • That is why we look at the average of several years and at deforestation alongside fire.

What each government did

The decisions that affected fire and clearing. The arrow shows whether the decision helped protect ↓ or weaken ↑ enforcement.

Lula, 1st and 2nd terms

2003 – 2010
  • ↓ PPCDAm, 2004. With Marina Silva at the Environment Ministry, the government creates the plan against Amazon deforestation, bringing together several ministries, satellites and enforcement. From 2004 to 2012, clearing falls 84%. Documented
  • ↓ Amazon Fund, 2008. Created by decree and managed by BNDES, it receives about R$3.4 billion, mostly from Norway, for projects against deforestation. Documented

Michel Temer

May 2016 – 2018
  • Clearing stable, at a higher level. Between 6,900 and 7,600 km² a year. We found no major federal measure, either way, that changed enforcement. Documented

Jair Bolsonaro

2019 – 2022
  • ↑ Amazon Fund frozen, 2019. The government abolishes the committee that approved projects. Norway and Germany suspend donations, and about R$3.2 billion sits idle. In 2022, the Supreme Court orders the fund reactivated. Documented
  • ↑ Fewer fines. In its first two years, Ibama issued an average of 2,610 infraction notices a year for crimes against flora in the Amazon, 43.5% fewer than the 2012–2018 average. Documented
  • ↑ Day of Fire, August 2019. Farmers in Novo Progresso, Pará, are accused of coordinating fires on the same day. The investigations were closed in 2024, with no one indicted. Allegation: coordination not proven

Is it true?

Half true

“Fires hit a record under Lula.” In 2024 Brazil had 278,000 hotspots, the most since 2010, in a year of record drought. But in 2025 the Amazon had the fewest hotspots in the series, and Lula 3's average is lower than Bolsonaro's.

True

“The worst period for fires was in Lula's first governments.” The 2003–2010 average was 302,000 hotspots a year, the highest of all. The record years were 2007 and 2004. But in the same period, deforestation fell 72%.

Half true

“Under Bolsonaro the Amazon burned like never before.” Deforestation rose 54% and fire was the highest of the governments after 2010. But fire numbers stayed well below the 2000s.

True

“Amazon deforestation rose under Bolsonaro.” It went from 7,536 km² in 2018 to 11,594 in 2022, with a peak of 13,038 in 2021: a 54% rise.

True

“Deforestation fell under Lula 3.” From 11,594 km² in 2022 to 5,731 in 2025: a 51% drop.

Half true

“Deforestation was highest under Lula 1 and 2.” The average was the highest, because he inherited the 2004 peak. But that was when it fell the most: 72% between 2003 and 2010.

True

“Bolsonaro froze the Amazon Fund.” In 2019 the government abolished the committee that approved projects, and donations stopped. The Supreme Court ordered it reactivated in 2022.

Not shown

“The Day of Fire was coordinated by farmers.” There were signs, but the investigations were closed with no one indicted.

In short

  • In fires, the highest average is Lula 1 and 2's, when clearing was at its peak. After that, Bolsonaro had the highest average and Dilma, the lowest. 2024 was the worst year since 2010 and 2025, the Amazon's best in the whole series.
  • In deforestation, the biggest drop was under Lula 1 and 2 and the biggest rise, under Bolsonaro. Under Lula 3 clearing fell again.
  • Fire depends a lot on the climate: years of strong drought, like 2024, go up under any government. But weak enforcement and high deforestation help fire spread.

Sources and limits of this reading

Hotspots are those of INPE's reference satellite, by month, from 1998 to September 2026, for Brazil and for the Amazon biome. A hotspot is a detected heat spot, not burned area; one large fire can generate many hotspots, and clouds and smoke can hide some. Deforestation is the PRODES rate for the Legal Amazon, which runs from August to July; each year's rate was assigned to the government in office for most of that period. Fire averages by government add up the months of each term; Temer starts in May 2016. Climate and drought weigh heavily on fire, so a difference between governments does not, on its own, prove it was their decision. The Cerrado, the Pantanal and other biomes also burn and do not have their own section here.
